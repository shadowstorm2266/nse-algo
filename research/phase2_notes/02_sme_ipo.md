## Q1. Current minimum application size, exact regulation text, notification date, and whether a "retail" category still exists

### Takeaway
As of 2026-10-31, ICDR Regulation 267(2) sets the SME IPO minimum application at **two lots, and above Rs 2 lakh**. This came from the ICDR (Amendment) Regulations, 2025, notification No. SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2025/233, which SEBI describes as notified on 4 March 2025, effective ("w.e.f.") 8 March 2025. The word "retail" was deleted from the SME chapter. The individual category is now "individual investors who applies for minimum application size". It gets at least 35% of the net offer in book-built issues and at least 50% in fixed-price issues, and is allotted by draw of lots. Nobody can apply with less than about Rs 2 lakh. Your understanding of the rule is correct. On dates: the board approved the changes on 18 Dec 2024, and the notification came in March 2025, not "around" March but on 3 to 8 March depending on which date you count (see below).

### Cited Findings
- **Exact current text, Reg 267(2)–(3) (PRIMARY):** "(2) The minimum application size shall be [two lots] per application[:] [Provided that the minimum application size shall be above 2 lakhs.] (3) The issuer shall invite applications in multiples of the [lot size]." Footnotes 489–492 say these words were "Substituted/Inserted by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 w.e.f. 08.03.2025". The words replaced were "one lakh rupees". — [SEBI ICDR Regulations 2018, consolidated, last amended 21 Mar 2026 (PDF)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774592300989.pdf) (landing page: [sebi.gov.in](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/mar-2026/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-issue-of-capital-and-disclosure-requirements-regulations-2018-last-amended-on-march-21-2026-_100581.html)), pp. 198–199.
- **Reg 267 Explanation (PRIMARY):** "'minimum application value' shall be with reference to the issue price of the specified securities and not with reference to the amount payable on application." So the rule that only 25% of the issue price need be paid on application (Reg 267(4)) does not lower the Rs 2 lakh threshold. — [Consolidated ICDR](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774592300989.pdf), p. 199.
- **Notification number and dates (PRIMARY, with a date discrepancy):** The amendment history in the consolidated ICDR lists "(ac) March 8, 2025 by the ... (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 vide notification No. SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2025/233". — [Consolidated ICDR](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774592300989.pdf), p. 481. SEBI's own Oct 2026 consultation paper says these Regulations were "notified on March 4, 2025" and "increased the minimum application size for SME IPOs from 1 lakh to 2 lakh". — [SEBI consultation paper, 14 Oct 2026 (PDF)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1791972839578.pdf), para 5.5.1.6.1. Secondary sources give the notification date as 3 March 2025 — [TMI listing, UNVERIFIED (secondary)](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/detail_act.asp?ID=33682). The best reading is: dated about 3 March, published in the Gazette about 4 March, in force 8 March 2025. I did not open the e-Gazette copy.
- **Board approval (PRIMARY):** The 208th SEBI Board meeting on 18 Dec 2024 (PR No. 36/2024, per the PDF) approved the SME package. The press release text does **not** list the application-size change among its bullets (2.1–2.9). The change appears in the board agenda memo the Board considered. — [SEBI PR 36/2024 (PDF)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/dec-2024/1734537931312.pdf); [press release page](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/dec-2024/sebi-board-meeting_90042.html). Note: WebFetch read the HTML page header as "37/2024", but the PDF and the NSE circular NSE/SME/65701 both say "PR No.36/2024".
- **Agenda memo, "proposals for approval of the Board" (PRIMARY):** "(i) minimum application size shall be two lots per application. (ii) Applications shall be in multiples of the lot size … (iii) Term 'retail individual investors' to be replaced with 'individual investors who applies for minimum application size'". Worked example: cap price Rs 20 → lot 6,000 → 2 × 6,000 × 20 = Rs 2,40,000. Range: "minimum application size for 2 lots shall be in range of Rs. 200200/- to Rs. 3,20,000/-". — [SEBI Board agenda memo, "Review of SME framework…" (PDF, 49 pp.)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), pp. 3–5.
- **Why "retail" was dropped (PRIMARY):** "in terms of ICDR definition, 'retail individual investor' means an individual investor who applies or bids … for a value of not more than two lakhs rupees, and … minimum application size shall be above two lakhs". The mainboard-style retail category is therefore impossible by construction. — [Agenda memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), p. 4.
- **Current allocation, Reg 253 (PRIMARY):**
  - Book-built issues, Reg 253(1): "a) not less than thirty five per cent. to individual investors who applies for minimum application size; b) not less than fifteen per cent. to non-institutional investors; c) not more than fifty per cent. to qualified institutional buyers, five per cent. of which shall be allocated to mutual funds". The word "retail" was omitted w.e.f. 08.03.2025.
  - Reg 253(2) splits the NII portion: one third for "more than two lots and up to … 10 lakhs" and two thirds for "more than 10 lakhs".
  - Fixed-price issues, Reg 253(3): "minimum fifty per cent. to individual investors who applies for minimum application size". — [Consolidated ICDR](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774592300989.pdf), pp. 190–192.
- **How the individual category is allotted (PRIMARY):** The agenda memo describes draw of lots as "currently applicable for retail investor category (i.e., draw of lots to allot minimum bid lot to applicants, in case of oversubscription and balance allotment on proportionate basis)". The same draw-of-lots method was extended to NIIs. Under Reg 268(3), proportionate allotment applies only to applicants *other than* individual investors applying for the minimum size, NIIs and anchors. — [Agenda memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), Annexure III para 2.1.3; [Consolidated ICDR](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774592300989.pdf), p. 199.
- **Minimum allottees (PRIMARY):** Reg 268(1): no allotment if allottees are fewer than "two hundred". This was substituted for "fifty" w.e.f. 08.03.2025. — [Consolidated ICDR](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774592300989.pdf), p. 199.
- **The Oct 2026 consultation describes the current rule the same way (PRIMARY):** "Regulation 267 presently prescribes a minimum application size of two lots per application, with the minimum application value being above 2 lakh … Regulation 253 presently provides for allocation … of not less than 35% to individual investors applying for the minimum application size". — [SEBI CP 14 Oct 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1791972839578.pdf), para 5.5.1.1.

### Inferences
- The individual category is effectively a lottery for one fixed ticket of about Rs 2.0–3.2 lakh. An individual who applies for more than two lots is classed as an NII, not as an "individual investor applying for the minimum size" (Reg 253(2)(a) wording, "more than two lots").
- The Board press release leaving out the application-size change suggests the Board may have approved the agenda's proposal without listing it, or settled it while drafting the notification. Either way, the notified text is the binding rule.

### Gaps
- I did not open the e-Gazette PDF of GN/2025/233, so I have not confirmed the exact date on its face (3 vs 4 March 2025).
- The Board minutes, which would record whether the application-size item was modified, were not checked.

## Q2. Every SEBI and exchange rule change for SME IPOs, 2024–2026, with numbers and dates

### Takeaway
There were three waves of change. (a) Exchange-level curbs in 2024: NSE's listing-day pre-open cap of +90% from 4 July 2024 (not March 2024), and NSE's FCFE test from 1 Sept 2024. (b) The SEBI package approved 18 Dec 2024 and notified March 2025, in force 8 March 2025, covering profitability, OFS, lock-in, GCP, promoter loans, monitoring agency, migration, minimum application and allottees. NSE applied the package to DRHPs filed on or after 19 Dec 2024. Several final numbers were *looser* than SEBI's own Nov 2024 proposals. (c) Fine-tuning in 2025–2026: NSE's −20% pre-open floor from 4 Aug 2025, NSE's FCFE revision of 20 Apr 2026, a SEBI pre-open call-auction consultation of 21 May 2026, and a pending SEBI consultation of 14 Oct 2026 (comments closed 4 Nov 2026). That last paper would partly reverse the 2025 tightening: Rs 1 lakh minimum, one-share trading lot, no compulsory market making. The trading lot for SME secondary-market trades has not changed since SEBI's 2012 circular.

### Inferences
- In effect, SEBI tightened the rules in Dec 2024 / Mar 2025 and is now partly reversing them (Oct 2026 paper). Anything about access for small investors could change in 2027. Re-verify before relying on any of it.
- The 90% cap (and, on NSE, the −20% floor) limits the *opening* price only. Price can move further later on listing day and afterwards, as press coverage notes. Listing-day statistics after July 2024 are therefore not comparable with earlier years.

## Q3. SEBI's stated reasons, and SEBI data on subscription, listing gains and manipulation

### Takeaway
SEBI's stated reasons for the higher application size were to protect smaller retail investors from riskier SME issues, to "attract investors with risk taking appetite", and the 14-year-old Rs 1 lakh threshold. SEBI cited fund diversion, circular trading and social-media tips. For NIIs it cited a SEBI study showing that proportionate allotment inflated "big-ticket" applications on the mainboard. In the SEBI documents I read, I found **no SEBI statistics on SME listing gains, subscription multiples or post-listing returns**. In Oct 2026 SEBI itself argues that the Rs 2 lakh minimum "may unnecessarily restrict access".

### Cited Findings
- **Rationale for the higher minimum (PRIMARY):** "It is observed that the retail individual participation has increased in SME IPO over last few years. Therefore, considering that SME IPOs tend to have higher element of risks, in order to protect the interest of smaller retail investors it is proposed to increase the minimum application size … As higher size will limit participation by smaller investors and shall attract investors with risk taking appetite, which will enhance the overall credibility of SME segment." Also: "This limit of Rs. 1 lakh was prescribed … over 14 years ago. In last 14 years Nifty and Sensex have grown by around 4.5 times". That was the basis for the rejected Rs 4 lakh option. — [Agenda memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), Annexure III paras 1.1.1–1.1.2.
- **Public comments on the application size (PRIMARY):** 99 comments: 62 strongly agree, 12 agree, 5 partially agree, 5 disagree, 15 strongly disagree. Figures reconstructed from a garbled table; they sum to 99. Many asked that the *trading* lot stay unchanged to protect liquidity. SEBI replied that the proposal did not change trading lots. — [Agenda memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), Annexure III paras 1.2.2–1.2.3.
- **SEBI study used for the NII change (PRIMARY; mainboard data, not SME):** After the 2022 mainboard NII change, "'Big Ticket NII Investors' (applying for more than 1 crore …) declined from around 626 applications per IPO in pre-period (April 21-Mar 22) to 20 applications per IPO in the post period (April 22-Dec 23) and representation … decreased from 98% to 8%". — [Agenda memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), Annexure III para 2.1.2. The study itself was not located.
- **Market size and fundraising (PRIMARY):**
  - FY 2023-24: 196 SME IPOs raised more than Rs 6,000 crore, the highest ever.
  - FY 2024-25 to 15 Oct 2024: more than Rs 5,700 crore from 159 SME IPOs.
  - Since inception: 565 NSE and 524 BSE exclusive SME listings, with 322 migrated to mainboard (as on 15 Oct 2024).
  — [Agenda memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), paras 2.3–2.4.
  - As on 31 Jul 2026: 1,496 entities listed on SME platforms since inception (including migrated), 365 migrated, Rs 40,222 crore raised, market cap Rs 4,70,984 crore. — [SEBI CP 14 Oct 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1791972839578.pdf), section 3.
  - 743 listed on NSE Emerge and 753 on BSE SME as on 31 Jul 2026. — [WG report](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/oct-2026/WG%20Report%20for%20Review%20of%20SME%20Provisions_p.pdf), p. 6.
- **Size of SME issuers (PRIMARY):** Average SME IPO market cap at issue price was Rs 144 crore (FY24), Rs 184 crore (FY25) and Rs 192 crore (FY26); maximums were Rs 720, 748 and 643 crore. — [WG report, Box 3.4](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/oct-2026/WG%20Report%20for%20Review%20of%20SME%20Provisions_p.pdf), p. 21.
- **SEBI's 2026 reasoning for reversing the minimum (PRIMARY):** The 2025 change was made "with the objective of limiting participation by smaller retail investors in view of the higher risks". With new safeguards in place, "retaining a 2 lakh minimum application value may unnecessarily restrict access to the SME capital market". The WG report adds: "the continued exclusion of domestic retail investors from meaningful participation in the SME Platform is increasingly difficult to reconcile with the evolution of India's capital markets". — [SEBI CP 14 Oct 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1791972839578.pdf), para 5.5.1.6.1; [WG report](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/oct-2026/WG%20Report%20for%20Review%20of%20SME%20Provisions_p.pdf), p. 6.
- **OFS rationale (PRIMARY):** SME IPOs could be 100% OFS. The agenda notes two pure-OFS SME IPOs and others where OFS exceeded 20%. "if promoters dilute too much of their stake too soon, it could undermine the company's growth potential". — [Agenda memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), para 3.2.4 and Annexure III para 4.

### Inferences
- The regulator's stated reason was risk and suitability, plus fraud and diversion. In the documents read, it was not a measured statistic on listing-day returns. Any link between "listing pops" and the rule change comes from press framing, not from SEBI documents I could verify.

### Gaps
- No SEBI study of SME subscription multiples, listing gains, flipping or pump-and-dump patterns was found. The Nov 2024 consultation paper itself was not downloaded and may contain such data. SEBI bulletins were not checked.
- No primary data on how individual-investor participation changed after Mar 2025.

## Q4. Evidence on SME IPO listing-day gains and post-listing returns, 2016–2026

### Takeaway
The peer-reviewed or refereed evidence covers mostly 2012–2018/2020. On average, SME IPOs listed above the issue price: about +8.7% raw mean on BSE SME, 2012–2017, n = 176. The best-known long-run study reports 12-month overperformance on BHAR, n = 375, 2012–2018. Every study is in-sample and ex-post, gross of costs and taxes, and measured at the issue level rather than the investor level. For 2024–2026 I found only secondary aggregator figures (UNVERIFIED). They show a sharp drop: mean listing gain about +57% in CY2024, about +11% in CY2025 and about +12% in 2026 to date, with medians near zero in 2025–2026. The +90% opening cap from July 2024 and the new rules from March 2025 break comparability across years.

### Cited Findings
- **Singh & Anand, "Do SME IPOs beat the market on listing day?", *Business Analyst* (SRCC, refereed college journal; ISSN 0973-211X), Vol. 41(2), pp. 107–135 (PRIMARY; publication year not shown in the extracted text, but it cites a 2020 paper, so 2020 or later).**
  - Period 23 Feb 2012 – 31 Mar 2017 (before 2016 for the most part). n = 176 BSE SME IPOs; about 98% were fixed-price issues.
  - Raw mean listing-day return **8.66%**. Market-adjusted vs Nifty and Sensex about 8.2%; vs the BSE SME IPO index more than 6%.
  - 5% trimmed means: 5.77% raw and 3.93% vs the SME index, so the average is driven by a few extreme cases.
  - 58 of 176 (33%) had negative Nifty-adjusted listing returns, i.e. about two-thirds beat Nifty. 72 (42%) were negative against the SME index.
  - In-sample. No costs or taxes. Return measured from issue price to listing-day close.
  - Why it may overstate: it assumes full allotment, but allotment is by lottery when oversubscribed. It is a small fixed-price sample. It is a different regime (Rs 1 lakh minimum, no pre-open cap).
  — [SRCC PDF](https://www.srcc.edu/system/files/5.pdf).
- **Arora & Singh, "The long-run performance of SME IPOs in India", *Journal of Asia Business Studies* 15(1):88–109, 2021 (online 15 Jun 2020), DOI 10.1108/JABS-10-2019-0305 (PRIMARY, abstract only).**
  - n = 375 SME IPOs, Feb 2012 – May 2018. Horizons of 3, 6, 9 and 12 months, using BHAR, CAR and wealth relatives.
  - Finds "long-run overperformance", significant under BHAR. Issue size and oversubscription relate *negatively* to BHAR.
  - Regressions use only 104 firms because "price data was unavailable".
  - In-sample. No costs or taxes. Benchmark not stated in the abstract. Numbers behind the paywall.
  - Why it may overstate: survivorship and data availability (only 104 of 375 had price data); illiquid SME prices; hot-market sample.
  — [Emerald abstract](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JABS-10-2019-0305/full/html).
- **Other studies, located by search only (not opened, so treated as not verified):**
  - Siwach, Kumar & Gupta, *Indian Journal of Finance* (2024): n = 383 SME IPOs, 2012–2020, with 12-month BHAR overperformance reported. — [IJF](https://indianjournalofentrepreneurship.com/index.php/IJF/article/view/173009).
  - Arora & Singh, *Global Business Review* 25(3) (2024): n = 403, 2012–2018. Underwriter reputation raises underpricing but has no long-run effect. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:globus:v:25:y:2024:i:3:p:632-655).
  - Dhamija & Arora, *Global Business Review* 18(6) (2017). — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/globus/v18y2017i6p1536-1551.html).
  - An IJRT paper reporting an about 18.3% initial return describes its 160 observations as an "illustrative dataset … calibrated". It is likely not real data, so do not use it. — [IJRT](https://ijrt.org/j/article/view/1653).
- **2024–2026 listing gains (UNVERIFIED, secondary aggregator; method unknown, likely issue price to listing-day close, both exchanges, gross of costs, no allotment adjustment):**
  - CY2024: 248 SME listings, mean +56.91%, median +37.50%.
  - CY2025: 269 SME listings, mean +11.15%, median +2.95%.
  - 2026 to 25 Oct: 183 SME listings, mean +12.18%, median +1.46%.
  — [Tapetide 2024](https://tapetide.com/insight/reports/ipo-listing-gains/2024), [2025](https://tapetide.com/insight/reports/ipo-listing-gains/2025), [2026](https://tapetide.com/insight/reports/ipo-listing-gains/2026). For CY2024, Mint citing Prime Database gives "around 60%" and Trendlyne gives about 56.7% at the open and 58.5% at the close (UNVERIFIED, from search snippets).
- **Post-listing (UNVERIFIED):** An article citing Prime Database says "nearly 74% of SME stocks are trading below their debut price" (FY26 listings). — [Multibagg, UNVERIFIED (secondary)](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/fy26-ipos-trading-below-listing-cmotq9cwyinsno50jb94fx6x5).
- **Fundraising, 2025–2026 (UNVERIFIED, secondary, citing NSE data):** FY25 NSE Emerge had 163 SME IPOs raising Rs 7,111 crore (average Rs 44 crore). — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/business/sme-ipos-gain-momentum-163-out-of-242-ipos-in-2024-25-were-from-small-businesses-nse-data). CY2025: 267 SME firms raised Rs 11,437 crore. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/fundraising-robust-2025-ipos-sme-record-qips-shrink-125123101041_1.html). H1 CY2026: about 80 SME IPOs raised about Rs 3,752 crore. — [IANS](https://ianslive.in/smes-raise-rs-3752-crore-via-around-80-ipos-in-jan-june-report--20260724120433).

### Inferences
- Arithmetic on the published Tapetide figures: the change in median listing gain from CY2024 to CY2025 is 2.95 − 37.50 = −34.55 percentage points, and in the mean 11.15 − 56.91 = −45.76 pp. The decline coincides with the July 2024 cap, the March 2025 rules and market conditions. The data cannot separate these causes.
- Issue-level averages overstate what an applicant earns. With draw-of-lots allotment, the expected return per application is roughly P(allotment) × listing gain, minus the opportunity cost of blocked funds. P(allotment) is low when an issue is heavily oversubscribed, and SEBI's study links oversubscription to weaker long-run BHAR. No study I found reports investor-level, after-tax, after-cost returns.
- Every figure is in-sample and gross of costs and taxes. None is out-of-sample or from live trading.

### Gaps
- No primary (NSE/BSE/SEBI) summary of SME listing-day returns for 2016–2026 was found.
- No primary figure for the share of SME IPOs listing at a premium after 2017. The SRCC study gives only market-adjusted shares.
- No peer-reviewed study covers the period after 2020. The BHAR magnitudes in Arora & Singh (2021) were not accessible.

## Q5. Can a resident individual with Rs 20,000 and one PAN apply to an SME IPO or trade SME shares?

### Takeaway
**Apply to an SME IPO: no.** The legal minimum is two lots and above Rs 2,00,000, about Rs 2.0–3.2 lakh in practice. Rs 20,000 is 6.25%–9.99% of that, and no smaller category exists. Even if the 14 Oct 2026 proposal is adopted, the minimum would be Rs 1,00,000, which is 5 times Rs 20,000. **Buy SME shares in the secondary market: in general no, under the current rules.** Trading must be in whole lots that were sized to be worth Rs 1.0–1.6 lakh at the issue price. One lot costs Rs 20,000 or less only when the share price has fallen far below the issue price (for example, at least 83% below for a Rs 100 issue). The proposed one-share trading lot would remove this barrier, but it is not in force.

### Cited Findings
- Minimum application: two lots, "above 2 lakhs" (Reg 267(2)). Minimum application value is measured at the issue price, not the amount paid (Reg 267 Explanation). — [Consolidated ICDR](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774592300989.pdf), pp. 198–199.
- Two-lot application range "Rs. 200200/- to Rs. 3,20,000/-"; one lot "ranges between Rs.1,00,100/- to 1,60,000/- depending on the price band". — [Agenda memo](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2025/1735725342588_1.pdf), pp. 3–4.
- The trading lot equals the IPO lot, and an exchange may not cut the lot size below the initial size while the price is below the issue price (2012 circular, paras 3 and 5). — quoted in [SEBI CP 14 Oct 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1791972839578.pdf), para 5.5.3.6.
- Proposed (pending): "four lots per application, each lot having a minimum value of 25,000", plus a one-share trading lot. — [SEBI CP 14 Oct 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1791972839578.pdf), paras 5.5.1.6.1, 5.5.3.3.1.
- 365 SME companies had migrated to the Main Board by 31 Jul 2026. — [SEBI CP 14 Oct 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1791972839578.pdf), section 3.

### Inferences (arithmetic on published figures; illustrative inputs, not real securities)
- **IPO, current rule:**
  - Required ≥ Rs 2,00,000 (strictly above). Ratio = 2,00,000 / 20,000 = **10×**.
  - Share of the minimum covered by Rs 20,000: from 20,000 / 3,20,000 = 6.25% to 20,000 / 2,00,200 = 9.99%.
  - SEBI's worked example: price Rs 20, lot 6,000, so 2 × 6,000 × 20 = Rs 2,40,000, which is 12× Rs 20,000.
- **IPO, proposed rule:** 4 × 25,000 = Rs 1,00,000, which is 1,00,000 / 20,000 = **5×**. Still not possible.
- **Secondary market at or near the issue price:** one lot ≈ Rs 1,00,100 to Rs 1,60,000, i.e. 5.0× to 8.0× Rs 20,000. Not possible.
- **Secondary market after a fall (formula: lot value = lot size × market price; affordable if lot size × P ≤ 20,000 minus charges):**
  - Illustration: issue price Rs 100 is in the "90–120" band, so the lot is 1,200 shares and one lot costs 1,200 × 100 = Rs 1,20,000 at issue.
  - The lot becomes affordable when P ≤ 20,000 / 1,200 = **Rs 16.67**. The fall needed is 1 − 16.67/100 = **83.3%** below the issue price, before brokerage and charges.
  - Illustration: issue price Rs 1,050 (lot 100). Affordable when P ≤ Rs 200, a fall of 1 − 200/1,050 = 81.0%.
  - So the only SME lots a Rs 20,000 investor could buy are those of issues that have collapsed in price. This applies only if the exchange has not since raised the lot size, which is unverified (see the Q2 gaps).
- **Migrated companies:** The 365 SME companies now on the Main Board presumably trade in single shares like other mainboard stocks. I did not verify their lot size in this session.
- **One PAN:** the individual category counts per applicant at a fixed minimum ticket. Nothing in the text read gives a smaller-ticket route for a single PAN.

### Gaps
- Whether NSE/BSE semi-annual lot reviews ever *increase* lot sizes after a price fall. This would change the secondary-market arithmetic.
- The mainboard market lot for migrated SME companies (assumed to be 1 share; not checked).

## Q6. Downloadable datasets for SME IPOs

### Takeaway
I found no single free, official, machine-readable dataset of SME IPOs with issue price, subscription and listing prices. The official sources are per-issue offer documents and exchange pages (NSE/BSE), and the exchanges limit scripted access. Free aggregator tables exist but are secondary and their methods are not documented. Paid databases (Prime Database, CMIE Prowess) are what academic studies typically use. I did not verify their prices.

### Cited Findings
- **SEBI regulatory and statistical documents (official, PDF, free, no login):** consolidated ICDR, board agenda papers, consultation papers and the WG report. The WG report and Oct 2026 paper include aggregate SME counts and market cap (as on 31 Jul 2026). — [Consolidated ICDR](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774592300989.pdf); [WG report](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/oct-2026/WG%20Report%20for%20Review%20of%20SME%20Provisions_p.pdf).
