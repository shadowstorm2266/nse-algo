## Q2. Retail subscription multiples and P(allotment) for a minimum-lot application

### Takeaway
When the retail book is oversubscribed, every valid retail application has about the same chance of one minimum lot, whatever size was bid. Measured P(allot) = successful retail allottees / valid retail applications. I read it from 30 basis-of-allotment (BoA) advertisements filed on NSE (PRIMARY; 2019-2025, mostly large and well-known issues). It ranges from **2.5%** (Happiest Minds 2020, Burger King 2020) to **100%** (issues where the retail book was undersubscribed: LIC, Hyundai, Swiggy, Tata Capital and others). The sample is heavily biased toward large issues, so its medians (8.4% for 2019-20, 50.4% for 2021-25) overstate P for a typical IPO. Small and mid-size issues that were heavily subscribed show P of 2.5% to 4%. A useful identity from the BoA tables: **P ≈ L̄ / x_RII**, where x_RII is the retail subscription multiple and L̄ is the average number of lots per retail application (measured at 1.57 to 2.27).

Caveats: the 0.219 retail share is SEBI's Apr 2021-Dec 2023 value share, and it mixes 35% and 10% quota issues. This is a ratio of averages. The per-IPO average P that matters for someone who applies to every IPO is higher, because hot IPOs draw more applications. But E[P x return] is lower than either, because hot IPOs have both low P and high returns (see Q6).
- With x_RII = 33.7x (2024 Prime average) and L̄ ≈ 1.7, an IPO at the average multiple gives P ≈ 1.7/33.7 ≈ 5%.

## Q3. Minimum application size, Rs 2 lakh retail ceiling, retail quota share, and 2025-2026 SEBI changes

### Takeaway
The mainboard minimum application value is set in the Rs 10,000-15,000 range. Lots in the BoA sample are Rs 13,527-14,980, so Rs 20,000 covers exactly one minimum lot at a time, never two. A retail individual bids at most Rs 2 lakh. Under the lottery, a one-lot bid has the same allotment chance as a Rs 2 lakh bid. The retail quota is at least 35% for profitability-route issues (Reg 6(1)) and at most 10% for Reg 6(2) issues. SEBI's 31 Jul 2025 proposal to cut retail to 25% for IPOs above Rs 5,000 crore was **dropped**. The Sept 2025 board memo says it is "no longer required to be pursued", because the 18 Aug 2025 minimum-public-offer proposal addressed large issuers instead. The board did approve anchor-book changes. I found no 2026 change to the mainboard retail quota or minimum application size.

### Cited Findings
- Minimum application size: "The issuer shall stipulate in the offer document the minimum application size in terms of number of specified securities which shall fall within the range of minimum application value of ten thousand rupees to fifteen thousand rupees." — ICDR 2018 text as reproduced by [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=548568) (UNVERIFIED reproduction; I did not check the regulation number against sebi.gov.in)

## Q4. Mechanics changes (dated), with emphasis on post-2021

### Takeaway
The changes that matter for a Rs 20,000 applicant are these. ASBA blocking has been mandatory since 1 Jan 2016, and blocked money stays in the bank account. UPI is the retail route through intermediaries, from Phase I in 2019, and since 1 May 2022 it can be used for bids up to Rs 5 lakh. Listing moved to T+3: voluntary for issues opening on or after 1 Sep 2023, mandatory from 1 Dec 2023, with unblocking completed by 4 pm on T+2. Together these shrink the time capital is locked per application to about 3 working days. From 1 Apr 2022 came NII lottery and split, a 50%/50% anchor lock-in at 30 and 90 days, and the RBI NBFC IPO-funding cap. They cut leveraged NII demand but left the retail lottery unchanged. The "one PAN, one application" rule is applied in practice. For SME issues there is an explicit exchange-level reject-upfront rule. I did not find the primary circular for mainboard.

### Inferences

## Q5. SEBI investor-behaviour study (Sept 2024) and the Indian IPO underpricing literature

### Takeaway
SEBI's study (144 mainboard IPOs, Apr 2021-Dec 2023, PAN-level exchange and depository data) shows that IPO allottees sell fast. Retail sold 42.7% of allotted value within a week, and individuals 50.2%. Individuals sold 67.6% within a week when first-week returns topped 20%, and 23.3% when returns were negative. Median listing-day return rises steeply with subscription, from -2% at 1-5x to 82% above 100x. The academic literature on Indian IPOs agrees that oversubscription is the main correlate of underpricing. The best-identified retail evidence comes from India's IPO lotteries (Anagol, Balasubramaniam and Ramadorai). I found no peer-reviewed paper that formally compares 2021-2025 with earlier years.

### Cited Findings
**SEBI, "Analysis of Investor Behavior in Initial Public Offerings (IPOs)"** (Department of Economic and Policy Analysis, Sept 2024, peer-reviewed by a SEBI working group). Sample: 144 mainboard IPOs listed Apr 2021-Dec 2023, 2.9 crore shareholders grossed across IPOs, 92.6 lakh unique shareholders. Holding snapshots run from LD-1 to LD+365. Exit is measured by value at issue price and weighted by allotted amount. In-sample, descriptive, no costs or taxes. — [PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1725270963087.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- About 54% (53.9%) of allotted value, excluding anchors, was sold within a week, and 72.6% within a year. Individuals sold 50.2% within a week. NII 63.3%, retail 42.7%, MFs 3.3%, banks 79.8%. — pp.2, 13-14
- Disposition effect: correlation between first-week listing gain and retail one-week exit = 0.7. Retail exited 61.9% within a week when first-week return was above 20%, against 42.7% on average. Individuals exited 67.6% in that case and 23.3% when returns were negative. — pp.15-16
- Individuals were 54.3% of gross listing-day selling by value, corporates 19.9% and banks 15.2%. In week 1, individuals were net sellers of about Rs 15,000 crore and MFs net buyers of about the same. — pp.19-20

- 39.3% of retail allotments went to Gujarat investors. Almost half the allotted demat accounts were opened in CY2021-23. — pp.2, 8
- Limits: the study reports no per-investor returns, no costs or taxes, no application-level (non-allottee) data, and exit is valued at issue price, not sale price.

## Q6. Expected value per minimum application and per year (arithmetic on published figures; not a backtest)

### Takeaway
The per-application EV depends mostly on how P(allot) and listing return move together, not on the period averages. Multiplying the average P by the average return independently gives Rs 75 to Rs 1,316 per application after 20% tax, depending on period and inputs. A version that keeps the link between the two (SEBI's subscription-bucket medians, with P per bucket set from the BoA sample) gives about **Rs 96 to Rs 280 per application** after tax, Rs 135 to Rs 371 at zero tax. At 20 to 50 applications a year, that is about **Rs 1,900 to Rs 14,000 a year**, or 10% to 70% of Rs 20,000. In-sample, the bucket that pays negative (-2% median, 1-5x subscribed) is the one where allotment is nearly certain.

### Cited Findings (inputs)
- Lot value L = **Rs 14,800**, typical of the BoA sample (Rs 13,527 to 14,980). — Q2 table (PRIMARY BoA ads)
- Listing return r, Tapetide open-to-issue, from Q1. 2018-20: count-weighted mean 17.43%, average of yearly medians 7.90%. 2021-26 YTD: mean 19.12%, median-average 9.70%. — [Tapetide](https://tapetide.com/insight/reports/ipo-listing-gains/2024) (UNVERIFIED)
- P(allot) from the BoA sample (Q2). 2019-20 median 8.35%, mean 22.28% (n=6). 2021-25 median 50.45%, mean 59.20% (n=23). Biased upward by large issues. Prime-implied aggregate about 15% (2024 13.8%, 2025 16.9%).
- SEBI buckets by subscription level (n, median listing return), Apr 2021-Dec 2023: >100x (17, 82%), 10-100x (75, 22%), 5-10x (10, 10%), 1-5x (40, -2%), undersubscribed (2, 2%). — [SEBI study, p.18](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1725270963087.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- Retail P by overall-subscription bucket in the BoA sample. Overall subscription is from each BoA's pre-rejection "Total" row; P is the retail figure. **>100x:** Happiest Minds 149.4x, 2.5%. Burger King 155.5x, 2.7%. Ujjivan SFB 101.4x, 3.2%. Devyani 115.6x, 3.8%. IRCTC 109.8x, 13.5%. Median **3.2%**. **10-100x:** Tata Tech 51.8x, 12.0%. Nykaa 45.7x, 13.4%. Campus 36.1x, 19.0%. Zomato 22.6x, 24.7%. Adani Wilmar 13.6x, 39.4%. SBI Cards 25.9x, 83.5%. Median **21.9%**. **1-5x:** IRFC 2.5x, 55.4%. Paytm 1.49x, 100%. Delhivery 1.33x, 100%. — BoA PDFs linked in Q2 (PRIMARY)
- Selling costs on sale value V = L(1+r)(1 - slippage). STT 0.1% of V, NSE transaction charge 0.00297% of V, SEBI fee Rs 10/crore (0.0001%), GST 18% on (transaction + SEBI), DP Rs 9 + 18% GST = Rs 10.62, brokerage zero, no stamp duty on the sell. Rates come from the repo's `config/costs.yaml`. The NSE rate of 0.00297% from 1 Oct 2024 (previously 0.00322%) is confirmed by [Zerodha bulletin, 28 Sep 2024](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/391488/revision-in-transactions-charges-from-1st-october-2024) and [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/stock-market-news/nse-and-bse-revise-their-transaction-fees-to-comply-with-sebi-circular-124092701207_1.html) (UNVERIFIED secondary; the NSE circular itself was not opened). STT 0.1% on a delivery sale was not re-verified against a primary source this session.
- Slippage is an assumption: 10 bps base, 50 bps stress. A sell order matched in the listing-day call auction executes at the equilibrium price, so slippage against the open may be near zero. Selling after the open into a 5% band (issues up to Rs 250 crore) may cost much more.
- Tax: (a) 0%, if gains fit inside the unused basic exemption (a separate researcher is checking whether this is allowed); (b) 20% STCG on the gain minus deductible transfer costs. STT is treated as non-deductible, which is an assumption for the tax researcher to verify. Positive and negative outcomes are netted within the year, consistent with STCL set-off, except in the bucket model, where only positive buckets are taxed (slightly conservative).

### Formula
EV_app = P x [ (V - L) - (STT + txn + SEBI + GST + DP) - Tax ], with V = L(1+r)(1-s).
Worked check at r = 10%, s = 10 bps: V = 14,800 x 1.10 x 0.999 = 16,263.7. STT 16.26 + txn 0.48 + SEBI 0.02 + GST 0.09 + DP 10.62 = **Rs 27.47**, plus slippage Rs 16.28. Total friction is about 0.30% of the lot.

### Inferences
- Plausible N: in 2018-2020 there were about 16-24 mainboard IPOs a year (Q1) with little overlap, so N is roughly 15-24. In 2021-2026 there were 39-111 a year, but many close in the same week. One Rs 15,000 lot cannot be blocked twice, so N is bounded by the number of distinct, non-overlapping bid windows. I did not count these; that needs the IPO calendar (Q8). My working range is 30-70.
- Medians times medians is not an expected value. The bucket model is the closer estimator, but it rests on the 2021-23 SEBI buckets and on assumed P for two buckets.
- Adverse selection is visible in-sample: the applications most likely to be filled (1-5x subscribed, P of 55-100%) had a -2% median listing return and a negative EV contribution.

- STT rate and the tax treatment of STT were not re-verified against primary sources in this session.

## Q7. Why these numbers could be overstated or may have decayed

### Takeaway
Six things push the Q6 figures up. Means are dominated by a few hot IPOs. P(allot) is lowest exactly where returns are highest. The BoA sample is biased toward large issues with high P. The yearly figures depend on the regime: 2021, 2023 and 2024 were strong, 2022 and 2025 were weak. Aggregator data may have survivorship gaps. The open or close price may not be fully achievable for small issues. Grey-market premium (GMP) data has no primary source.

### Cited Findings
- **Mean is far above the median every year.** Tapetide 2024: mean +28.17% against median +15.94%. 2021: +30.28% against +11.19%. 2025: +9.08% against +4.22%. — [Tapetide 2024](https://tapetide.com/insight/reports/ipo-listing-gains/2024), [2021](https://tapetide.com/insight/reports/ipo-listing-gains/2021), [2025](https://tapetide.com/insight/reports/ipo-listing-gains/2025) (UNVERIFIED). SEBI: 26 of 144 IPOs listed more than 50% up. — [SEBI study, p.5](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1725270963087.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **High returns go with low P.** The 17 IPOs subscribed above 100x had a median listing return of 82%. In the BoA sample, retail P in that bucket was 2.5% to 3.8% for issues other than IRCTC. — [SEBI study, p.18](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1725270963087.pdf); BoA PDFs ([Happiest Minds](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ADV_INE419U01012_16SEP2020.pdf), [Burger King](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ADV_INE07T201019_11DEC2020.pdf), [Devyani](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ADV_INE872J01023_13AUG2021.pdf), [Ujjivan SFB](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ADV_INE551W01018_11DEC2019.pdf)) (PRIMARY)
- **Rising participation.** Average retail applications per IPO rose from 4.05 lakh (2019) to 18.87 lakh (2024), then 14.99 lakh (2025). — Prime via [Outlook](https://outlookindia.com/business/business-news-ipo-fundraising-2021-63-companies-mop-up-rs-118-lakh-crore-from-markets-so-far-news-406418) and [Industrial Economist](https://industrialeconomist.com/?p=18170) (UNVERIFIED). Almost half the allotted demat accounts were opened in CY2021-23. — [SEBI study, p.8](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1725270963087.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Regime dependence.** Prime close-based means: 2020 44%, 2021 32%, 2022 11%, 2023 29%, 2024 30%, 2025 10%. In 2025, 54 of 102 IPOs traded above issue price on 24 Dec 2025. Prime's MD said a strong IPO year "has typically been followed by a lull of two to three years". — [Industrial Economist](https://industrialeconomist.com/?p=18170), [Business Standard 2023 year-end](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/ipo-fundraising-drops-17-in-2023-40-gave-a-return-of-over-10-on-listing-124010300366_1.html) (UNVERIFIED)
- **Survivorship and coverage.** Mainboard counts differ by source: 2018 has 16 (Trendlyne) against 24 (Tapetide); 2024 has 90 (NSE), 91 (Prime), 92 (Trendlyne) and 93 (Tapetide). — Q1 sources (UNVERIFIED, except NSE's figure as reported by [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/ipo/nse-leads-asia-in-ipos-with-268-listings-in-2024-across-mainboard-sme-125010300761_1.html))
- **Achievability of the listing price.** Listing day opens with a one-hour call auction. Issues up to Rs 250 crore then trade in a 5% band around the equilibrium price, and larger issues in a 20% band. — [TaxGuru reproduction of the SEBI 2012 circular](https://taxguru.in/sebi/formulation-price-bands-day-trading-pursuant-ipo-re-listing.html) (UNVERIFIED). SEBI proposed in 2026 that an auction count only with at least 5 unique PAN buyers and sellers, which points to concern about thin or manipulated discovery. — [JSA Prism, Jun 2026](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/JSA-Prism-Securities-Law-June-2026-Price-discovery-mechanism-review.Final_.pdf) (UNVERIFIED). Individuals were 54.3% of listing-day gross selling, so allottees are competing to sell. — [SEBI study, p.19](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1725270963087.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Rejections.** Paytm had 1,062,314 retail applications before technical rejections and 993,792 valid ones after, a 6.5% drop. — [BoA Paytm](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ADV_INE982J01020_17NOV2021.pdf) (PRIMARY). A rejected application has P = 0. Under UPI, an unaccepted mandate counts as a non-bid.

### Inferences
- **Grey-market premium.** Applicants and media often decide using GMP. GMP is an unregulated, informal quote with no primary record, so any strategy filtered on GMP cannot be verified historically. Not used in these notes.
- **Selection inside an application rule.** If one can apply to only about 30-70 of about 100 IPOs a year, the choice rule (for example, the most-subscribed one on each closing day) changes the EV a lot. Picking hot issues raises r but lowers P, which pushes toward the "low P" bucket figures.
- **Overstatement from the sample design.** Q2's P distribution and the "BoA P" rows in Q6 use mostly large, well-known issues, so they are upper-biased. The SEBI bucket results with low P are the more conservative figures.
- **Regime decay.** 2025 and 2026 YTD medians (4.2%, 6.7%) are well below 2023-24 (16.4%, 15.9%). Results built on 2021-24 may not carry forward.
- **Open versus close.** Tapetide measures to the open. For small issues in a 5% band, a holder who misses the auction can be stuck if the stock hits the lower band. In the weaker years, more IPOs closed in loss than opened in loss (Trendlyne 2025: 22 opened in loss, 35 closed in loss).

### Gaps
- No primary data on how often listing-day call auctions fail to discover a price, or on the depth at the equilibrium price.
- No GMP history from any verifiable source.
- No study quantifying the winner's curse for Indian retail in the post-2021 period.

## Q8. Downloadable datasets for testing IPO applications

### Takeaway
A full test needs, for every mainboard IPO, the issue price, lot size, retail quota, retail valid applications, successful allottees, retail subscription, overall subscription, open and close dates, and listing-day OHLC including the call-auction price. The NSE `ADV_` archive of BoA advertisements (free, PDF, about 2019 onward) has the allotment fields. The friction is that you must know each IPO's ISIN and filing date, and the tables are two-column PDFs. Pre-2019 BoA data was not found on NSE.

### Cited Findings
- **NSE BoA advertisements (PRIMARY).** `https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ADV_<ISIN>_<DDMONYYYY>.pdf`, for example [Bajaj HFL](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ADV_INE377Y01014_13SEP2024.pdf). Format: PDF, one page, two-column newspaper layout. Fields: bids before rejection by category (applications, shares, times subscribed); retail, NII and QIB tables after rejection (applications per lot bucket, shares applied, allotment ratio, shares allotted); successful allottees; anchor details. Coverage: 26 of 26 probed issues from 2019-2025 were found, and 0 of 10 from 2016-2018. Cost: free. Friction: no index. You need the ISIN and the filing date, which is usually one day before listing (sometimes two to three with weekends), so I probed listing date minus 1 to 7 days. Text extraction: `pdftotext -raw` works, but row order is sometimes scrambled (Swiggy, Tata Technologies). Rate limits are unknown; about 4 requests a second worked.
- **SEBI offer documents (PRIMARY).** RHPs and prospectuses at [sebi.gov.in filings](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/home/HomeAction.do?doListing=yes&sid=3&ssid=15&smid=11) give price band, lot size, reservation shares and whether the issue uses Reg 6(1) or 6(2). Free PDFs, hundreds of pages each; fields must be extracted by hand or with a parser. (Listing URL from SEBI's site structure; not opened this session.)
- **SEBI study aggregates (PRIMARY).** The [Sept 2024 PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1725270963087.pdf) gives aggregate tables only. The PAN-level data is not public.
- **Listing-day OHLC.** NSE daily CM bhavcopy archives (open, high, low, close; free, CSV in ZIP, from nsearchives.nseindia.com) and the BSE equivalent. Not fetched this session; the archive path is not verified here. The listing open equals the call-auction equilibrium price.
- **UNVERIFIED aggregators (use to locate data only):** [Tapetide yearly listing gains](https://tapetide.com/insight/reports/ipo-listing-gains/2024) (HTML, 2018 onward; mean, median and counts per year; free). [Trendlyne IPO screener](https://trendlyne.com/ipo/screener/year-2024/mainline/) (HTML, 2018 onward; open and close averages; per-IPO table and export button; free tier). [Chittorgarh reports](https://www.chittorgarh.com/report/ipo_basis_of_allotment_list/7/mainline/?year=2024) (per-year subscription, listing gain, "No. of Retail Applications" and BoA lists; tables load via JavaScript and were empty in a plain curl fetch). Prime Database (primedatabase.com; paid; the source behind most year-end news figures; cost not checked).
- **Registrar sites.** KFin Technologies, MUFG Intime (formerly Link Intime) and Bigshare host per-PAN allotment-status lookups, usually behind a captcha. I did not check whether they host BoA PDFs for download.
- **Academic.** [Ritter's India series](https://site.warrington.ufl.edu/ritter/files/IPOs-India.pdf) (chart only, 1990-2025, mainboard only from 2004). The Anagol et al. lottery data is not public, as far as checked.

### Inferences
- A minimum pipeline: (1) get the mainboard IPO list with ISIN and listing date from the NSE or BSE past-issues pages or the bhavcopy; (2) probe `ADV_<ISIN>_<date>` for listing date minus 1 to 7 days; (3) parse the retail table (valid applications, allottees, minimum-lot ratio) and the pre-rejection "Total" row (overall subscription); (4) join to the bhavcopy for listing-day open and close. This gives per-IPO P_i and r_i, so E[P x r] can be computed directly rather than with the bucket assumption in Q6. For 2016-2018, BSE notices or newspaper archives would be needed.
- The probe script and parsers used here are in the scratchpad (`r1_ipo/scripts/probe.py`, `parse_boa.py`). Parser output needs manual checks: several fields failed to parse, as noted in Q2.

### Gaps
- No free, primary, machine-readable dataset of retail application counts per IPO was found. The NSE bid-details pages show live subscription by category but were not tested for history or API access (cookies are probably required).
- Whether BSE hosts pre-2019 BoA advertisements, and at what URL, was not checked.
- Prime Database pricing and licence terms were not checked.
