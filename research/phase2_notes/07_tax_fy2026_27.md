## Which law applies to FY 2026-27, the "tax year", section mapping, and what the Finance Act 2026 changed

### Takeaway
FY 2026-27 is governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025, which came into force on 1 April 2026. It replaces "previous year / assessment year" with a single "tax year". The old 111A, 112A and 87A are now sections 196, 198 and 156. The Finance Act 2026 left slab rates, the 20% STCG rate, the 12.5% LTCG rate, the Rs 1.25 lakh LTCG exemption and cash-equity STT unchanged. It raised STT on F&O only, moved buybacks back to capital-gains treatment from 1 April 2026 (with an extra tax for promoters), and moved the return due date for non-audit business cases to 31 August.

### Cited Findings
- **Commencement.** Section 1(3) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 says "Save as otherwise provided in this Act, it shall come into force on the 1st April, 2026". The Act received assent on 21 Aug 2025 as No. 30 of 2025. — [Income-tax Act 2025, e-Gazette, 21 Aug 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Tax year.** Section 3(1) defines "tax year" as "the twelve months period of the financial year commencing on the 1st April". Section 3(2) shortens it for a business or source of income that starts during the year. Tax year 2026-27 is therefore 1 Apr 2026 to 31 Mar 2027. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Two Acts run side by side.** FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) is still assessed under the 1961 Act. The Memorandum lists separate rate parts: rates "for the assessment year 2026-27 for the purposes of the Income-tax Act, 1961" and "tax rates under Part I-B of the First Schedule applicable for the tax year 2026-27" under the 2025 Act. The Finance Act 2026 amends both Acts. — [Memorandum, Finance Bill 2026](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf) (PRIMARY, 1 Feb 2026)
- **Section mapping, read from the 2025 Act text (PRIMARY; old-to-new correspondence is my inference from matching wording):**
  - 111A → **s.196**: 20% on STCG from STT-chargeable sales of equity shares, equity-oriented fund units and business trust units (s.196(1)). Shortfall rule in s.196(2). IFSC carve-out in s.196(3). Chapter VIII deductions not allowed against this STCG (s.196(4)).
  - 112 → **s.197**: 12.5% on LTCG, with its own shortfall rule in s.197(2).
  - 112A → **s.198**: 12.5% on STT-paid equity LTCG "exceeding 125000" (s.198(2)(a)). Shortfall rule in s.198(3). Rebate bar in s.198(7).
  - 87A → **s.156**: s.156(1) old-regime rebate. s.156(2) new-regime rebate with marginal relief. s.156(3) caps the rebate.
  - 115BAC → **s.202**: the default new regime. The Memorandum calls the opt-out "the option under 202(4)".
  - 2(22)(f) → **s.2(40)(f)**: buyback payments as dividend. Omitted by the Finance Act 2026.
  - 46A → **s.69**: capital gains on a company buying back its own shares.
  - 43(5) → **s.66(31)**: "speculative transaction". 28 Expl. 2 → **s.26(3)**: a speculation business is "deemed to be distinct and separate from any other business".
  - 70/71/72/73/74/80 → **ss.108/109/112/113/111/121**: set-off and carry-forward of losses.
  - 64(1A) → **s.99(1)(c)**, with s.99(2)(b) deciding which parent. 10(32) → **Schedule III, Table Sl. No. 17** (Rs 1,500 per minor child).
  - 139 → **s.263**. 234F → **s.428**. 208 → **s.404**. 194 → **s.393(1) Table Sl. No. 7**. 197A (Form 15G/15H) → **s.393(6)**.
  - 48 (STT not deductible from capital gains) → **s.72(3)(b)**. 36(1)(xv) (STT deductible as a business expense) → **s.32(k)**. 44AB → **s.63**. 44AD → **s.58**. 2(42A) → **s.2(101)**.
  - Source for all of the above: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf)
- **Holding period.** Under s.2(101)(a), a capital asset is short-term if held for not more than 24 months. Section 2(101)(b) substitutes "twelve months" for a listed security, a UTI unit, a unit of an equity-oriented fund or a zero-coupon bond. Listed equity must therefore be held more than 12 months to give LTCG. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Rates unchanged for tax year 2026-27.**
  - New-regime slabs in s.202(1): nil up to 4,00,000; 5% to 8 lakh; 10% to 12 lakh; 15% to 16 lakh; 20% to 20 lakh; 25% to 24 lakh; 30% above. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
  - The Memorandum repeats the same table "with effect from tax year 2026-27". It also gives old-regime (Part I-B) slabs: nil to 2,50,000, 5% to 5 lakh, 20% to 10 lakh, 30% above. For residents aged 60-79 the nil band is 3 lakh; for 80+ it is 5 lakh. — [Memorandum](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf) (PRIMARY)
  - Section 196 still says 20% and section 198 still says 12.5% above 1,25,000. A text search of the Finance Act 2026 found no amendment to ss.156, 196 or 198. — [Finance Act 2026 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf)
- **Cess and surcharge.** Finance Act 2026 s.3(15) and s.3(16) levy "Health and Education Cess on income-tax" at 4% of income-tax plus surcharge. Individual surcharge starts only above Rs 50 lakh of total income. Surcharge on dividend income and on s.196/197/198 capital gains is capped at 15%. — [Finance Act 2026 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf); [Memorandum](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf) (PRIMARY)

  - Finance Act 2026 s.42 substitutes s.69(2)-(3). Where the buyback is made "in accordance with the provisions of section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013" and the holder is a promoter, an additional income-tax applies:

  - Sources: [Finance Act 2026 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf); [Income-tax Act 2025 s.69](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY).   - The Lok Sabha passed the Bill on 25 Mar 2026 with 30+ amendments. One of them limited the promoter tax to buybacks under s.68 of the Companies Act. — [BDO alert, 27 Mar 2026](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/insights/alerts-updates/direct-tax-alert-key-amendments-to-the-finance-bill-2026-as-passed-by-lok-sabha) UNVERIFIED (secondary). ### Inferences
- For an NSE cash-equity bot in tax year 2026-27:
  - CNC sells held 12 months or less give s.196 STCG.
  - CNC sells held more than 12 months give s.198 LTCG.
  - MIS round trips are s.66(31) speculative transactions and are taxed as speculation business income, which s.26(3) treats as a separate business.

## Slabs and basic exemption (new vs old regime), and whether the rebate (old 87A, now s.156) can be used against special-rate STCG/LTCG

### Takeaway
New regime (the default): nil tax up to Rs 4 lakh, and a rebate of up to Rs 60,000 when total income is Rs 12 lakh or less. Old regime (opt-out), under age 60: nil up to Rs 2.5 lakh, and a rebate of up to Rs 12,500 when total income is Rs 5 lakh or less.

For tax year 2026-27 the new-regime rebate cannot reduce tax on s.196 STCG or s.198 LTCG. Section 156(3) caps it at tax "as per the rates provided in section 202(1)", and s.198(7) separately bars it against LTCG. Taxpayers won the rebate-on-STCG dispute before the ITAT for AY 2024-25 and AY 2025-26 because the old 87A had no express bar. That reasoning does not carry over: an express bar applies from AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26) under the Finance Act 2025, and s.156(3) carries it into the 2025 Act.

### Cited Findings
- **New regime, s.202(1).** Slabs as listed in the first section, with a nil band up to 4,00,000. Applies "unless the person exercises the option in the manner provided under sub-section (4)". — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY, effective 1 Apr 2026)
- **Exemptions lost under the new regime.** Section 202(2)(a)(i) computes new-regime income "without any exemption or deduction under Schedule III (Table: Sl. No. 5 or 6 or 7 or 8 or 11 or 17)". Sl. No. 17 is the Rs 1,500 minor-child exclusion (see the minors section). — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Old regime (opt-out), tax year 2026-27, from Part I-B of the First Schedule.**
  - Under 60: nil up to 2,50,000; 5% to 5,00,000; 20% to 10,00,000; 30% above.
  - Residents aged 60-79: nil up to 3,00,000.
  - Residents aged 80+: nil up to 5,00,000.
  - Source: [Memorandum, Finance Bill 2026](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf) (PRIMARY, 1 Feb 2026)
- **s.156(1), old-regime rebate.** A resident individual gets "a deduction of 100% of income-tax payable or 12500, whichever is less ... if such total income does not exceed 500000". There is no cap tied to special rates. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **s.156(2), new-regime rebate.**
  - (a) If "the income does not exceed twelve lakh rupees": 100% of income-tax payable or 60,000, whichever is less.
  - (b) If total income exceeds 12 lakh: marginal relief equal to the amount by which tax exceeds the income above 12 lakh.
  - **s.156(3):** "The deduction under sub-section (2), shall not exceed income-tax payable as per the rates provided in section 202(1)."
  - Source: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **s.198(7), LTCG.** "Where the total income of an assessee includes any long-term capital gains referred to in sub-section (1), the rebate under section 156 shall be allowed from the income-tax on the total income as reduced by tax payable on such capital gains." Section 196 has no equivalent sub-section; s.156(3) does that job for the new regime. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Finance Act 2025, 1961 Act, effective 1 Apr 2026 (AY 2026-27 = FY 2025-26).** Section 20 amended 87A:
  - 7 lakh became 12 lakh, and 25,000 became 60,000;
  - a new proviso was inserted: "the deduction under the first proviso, shall not exceed the amount of income-tax payable as per the rates provided in sub-section (1A) of section 115BAC".
  - Source: [Finance Act 2025 (No. 7 of 2025), assent 29 Mar 2025](https://thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Acts/Finance%20Act,%202025.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Litigation, AY 2024-25 (FY 2023-24, old 87A first proviso: 7 lakh / 25,000).** All of the following is PRIMARY, from the [ITAT Agra order](https://itat.gov.in/public/files/upload/1756901560-YJpjWc-1-TO.pdf) of 3 Sep 2025, which reproduces the Ahmedabad order in full:

  - The CIT(A) relied on the Finance Bill 2025 Memorandum to uphold the denial.
  - The Bombay High Court, in *Chamber of Tax Consultants v. DGIT (Systems)*, PIL (L) No. 32465 of 2024, judgment dated 24.01.2025, "directed the Department to permit the assessees to make claims for rebate under section 87A". It "refrained from adjudicating on the legal correctness of such claims" and left merits to quasi-judicial authorities.

Its reasoning: 87A and 111A contain no express bar, unlike 112A(6), and the Finance Bill 2025 amendment is prospective, which "implies that the existing law ... does not contain such a restriction".
  - ITAT Agra, *Pramod Kumar Dubey v. ITO*, ITA 314/AGR/2025 (3 Sep 2025), followed Ahmedabad and allowed the rebate.

### Inferences
- **New regime, tax year 2026-27.** The rebate can only reduce tax computed at s.202(1) slab rates, on intraday, dividend, interest or salary income. Tax on s.196 STCG (20%) and s.198 LTCG (12.5%) is paid in full once the shortfall rule (next section) is exhausted. This is why STCG starts being taxed at Rs 4 lakh of total income in the tables, while intraday income stays nil up to Rs 12 lakh.
- **Old regime, tax year 2026-27.** s.156(1) contains no s.156(3)-style cap. On the plain text, the Rs 12,500 rebate (total income up to 5 lakh) can therefore reduce tax on s.196 STCG, but not on s.198 LTCG because of s.198(7). The old-regime rows in the tables use this reading.
- **The AY 2024-25 and AY 2025-26 ITAT wins do not apply to tax year 2026-27.** They rested on the absence of an express bar, and s.156(3) is an express bar. I found no reported challenge to s.156(3).
- **Eligibility for the rebate.** "Income" in s.156(2)(a) appears to mean total income including STCG and LTCG. On that reading, special-rate gains count toward the 12 lakh limit even though the rebate cannot reduce tax on them. This matches the old 87A, which used "total income".
- **One more ambiguity.** For total income above 12 lakh, s.156(2)(b) speaks of "income-tax payable on such total income". It is unclear whether that includes s.196/198 tax. The s.156(3) cap at slab-rate tax makes this matter only when there is slab-rate tax to relieve.

- I did not read the CBDT Circular 13/2025 text or any AY 2025-26 ITAT order in full.

- I found no ruling or CBDT guidance on s.156(3) for tax year 2026-27, and no CPC/ITR utility logic for tax year 2026-27 (those ITR forms are filed from April 2027).

## Basic exemption shortfall rule for STCG and LTCG (old 111A proviso / 112A proviso): equivalent in the 2025 Act, both regimes, non-residents

### Takeaway
Yes. For a **resident** individual (or HUF), s.196(2) for STCG and s.198(3) for equity LTCG reduce the special-rate gain by the amount by which the rest of total income falls short of "the maximum amount which is not chargeable to income-tax". So STCG and LTCG can absorb an unused basic exemption. The wording is not tied to a regime, so the limit is Rs 4 lakh under the new regime and Rs 2.5 lakh under the old regime (under 60). Non-residents cannot use it, because each provision is limited to an individual or HUF "being a resident".

### Cited Findings
- **s.196(2), STCG.** "In the case of an individual or a Hindu undivided family, being a resident, where the total income, as reduced by short-term capital gains computed under sub-section (1), is below the maximum amount which is not chargeable to income-tax, then,-- (a) such short-term capital gains shall be reduced by the amount by which the total income as so reduced falls short of the maximum amount which is not chargeable to income-tax; and (b) the tax on the balance of such short-term capital gains shall be computed at the rate as applicable in sub-section (1)(i)" [20%]. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY, effective 1 Apr 2026)
- **s.198(2)-(3), equity LTCG.**
  - s.198(2)(a): "income-tax calculated on such long-term capital gains exceeding 125000 at the rate of 12.5%".
  - s.198(3): for a resident individual or HUF, where total income reduced by LTCG "is below the maximum amount which is not chargeable to income-tax", the LTCG "shall be reduced by the amount by which the total income as so reduced falls short", and the balance is taxed "at the rate as referred to in sub-section (2)".
  - s.197(2) has the same rule for other LTCG.
  - Source: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **The limits.** The nil band is 4,00,000 under s.202(1) (new regime). Under the old regime it is 2,50,000 (under 60), 3,00,000 (60-79) and 5,00,000 (80+). — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf); [Memorandum](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **No Chapter VIII deductions against these gains.** Under s.196(4) and s.198(6), Chapter VIII deductions are allowed only from gross total income as reduced by these gains. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **The ITAT facts show the rule working.** In the Ahmedabad case, AY 2024-25, total income was Rs 4,27,635, of which Rs 3,79,559 was 111A STCG. Tax of Rs 13,320 arose "solely on account of STCG under section 111A ... chargeable at 15%". That figure is consistent with STCG being taxed after the shortfall to the then 3 lakh new-regime limit. — [ITAT Agra order reproducing ITAT Ahmedabad](https://itat.gov.in/public/files/upload/1756901560-YJpjWc-1-TO.pdf) (PRIMARY, 3 Sep 2025)

### Inferences
- **Formula, new regime, resident.** Let O be slab-rate income (salary after deductions, interest, dividends, intraday). Then:
  - shortfall S = max(0, 4,00,000 − O);
  - STCG tax = 20% × max(0, STCG − S);
  - equity LTCG tax = 12.5% × max(0, LTCG − 1,25,000 − S_left), where S_left is whatever shortfall the STCG did not use;
  - add 4% cess.
- **Slab income uses the exemption first.** The shortfall is measured on total income *as reduced by* the special-rate gain. Intraday (speculative) income, dividends and interest therefore use up the basic exemption before STCG does. With Rs 3 lakh of other income, only Rs 1 lakh of shortfall is left for STCG.
- **Order between STCG and LTCG.** Neither s.196(2) nor s.198(3) says which absorbs the shortfall first when both exist. The tables apply it to STCG first (20% > 12.5%). This is an assumption, and I did not verify CPC practice.
- **Both regimes.** The provisions refer to the regime-dependent "maximum amount which is not chargeable", so they work under both. Under the old regime the shortfall is smaller (2.5 lakh), but the Rs 12,500 rebate may still cover s.196 tax (see the rebate section).
- **Non-residents and residents but not ordinarily resident.** Non-resident individuals get no shortfall: their s.196 tax runs from the first rupee of STCG. Residents who are "not ordinarily resident" are still "resident", so on the text the rule appears to apply to them; I did not see this confirmed.

### Gaps
- I found no CBDT guidance or ruling on the order in which STCG and LTCG absorb the shortfall under the 2025 Act.

## Intraday (MIS) gains as speculative business income: rate, basic exemption, ITR form, audit and presumptive tax, losses

### Takeaway
Intraday equity round trips (no delivery) are "speculative transactions" under s.66(31). They are taxed as a separate speculation business under "Profits and gains of business or profession", at ordinary slab rates. They use the basic exemption normally and qualify for the s.156 rebate. Under the new regime, intraday income up to Rs 12 lakh (with no other income) therefore attracts nil tax. Losses can only be set off against speculation profits and carried forward 4 years, and only if the return is filed by the due date. A tax audit applies only above Rs 1 crore of turnover (Rs 10 crore if cash is 5% or less). The due date for non-audit business cases is 31 August.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition, s.66(31).** "Speculative transaction" means "a transaction in which a contract for the purchase or sale of any commodity, including stocks and shares, is periodically or ultimately settled otherwise than by the actual delivery or transfer of the commodity or scrips". Exclusions include (a) a specified derivative transaction under clause (33), and (c) hedging contracts by a dealer or investor. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Separate business, s.26(3).** "Where speculative transactions carried on by an assessee are of such nature to constitute a business, the business (herein referred to as speculation business) shall be deemed to be distinct and separate from any other business." This sits in Part D, "Profits and gains of business or profession". — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **STT deductible here.** Section 32(k) allows "the amount paid as securities transaction tax" as a business deduction where the transactions are in the course of business and the income is taxed as business income. For capital gains, by contrast, s.72(3)(b) disallows "any sum paid as securities transaction tax". — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Losses, s.113.**
  - s.113(1): a speculation loss "shall be set off only against profits and gains of another speculation business".
  - s.113(2)-(3): unabsorbed loss is carried forward against speculation profits for no "more than four tax years".
  - s.121: no loss is carried forward under s.111(1), 112(1), 113(2), 114(2) or 115(2) unless it was "determined in pursuance of a return filed under section 263(1)", the on-time return.
  - Source: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Set-off with other heads.** Section 109(1)(a) stops business losses being set off against salary. Section 109(2): "the loss under the head 'Capital gains' shall not be set off against income under any other head". So a capital loss cannot reduce intraday income, and s.113 stops an intraday loss reducing capital gains. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Tax audit, s.63 Table Sl. 1.** Required if business "total sales, turnover or gross receipts ... exceed ... one crore rupees". The limit is "ten crore rupees" where cash receipts and cash payments are each 5% or less. Table Sl. 2 adds an audit trigger for presumptive cases that declare less than the deemed profit. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Presumptive tax, s.58.** The "eligible assessee" under s.58(11)(a) is a resident individual, HUF or non-LLP firm. It excludes those carrying on a specified profession, earning commission or brokerage, or carrying on agency business. Speculation business is not expressly excluded. Section 58(7)-(8) imposes a 5-year lock-out, and requires books plus an audit where total income exceeds the basic exemption, if a taxpayer leaves the scheme. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Due date.** 31 August after the tax year for business income without audit, so 31 Aug 2027 for tax year 2026-27. The Memorandum says ITR-1 and ITR-2 filers keep 31 July. — [Finance Act 2026 s.66 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf); [Memorandum](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf) (PRIMARY)

### Inferences
- **Rate and rebate.** Intraday income is slab-rate income, so s.156(2) applies to it in full. In the new regime, with no other income, intraday profit up to Rs 12,00,000 gives nil tax. Marginal relief then phases tax in until about Rs 12,70,588 (where 60,000 + 15% × excess = excess). With Rs 3 lakh of other income, nil tax runs up to Rs 9 lakh of intraday profit. See the tables.
- **Order of exemption use.** Intraday income uses the basic exemption ahead of CNC STCG (see the shortfall section). A bot running both MIS and CNC strategies would see its STCG taxed sooner than the STCG-only figures suggest.
- **Audit unlikely for a small account.** On a Rs 20,000 account, turnover is far below Rs 1 crore on any common measure, so a tax audit is very unlikely unless s.58 presumptive rules are triggered.
- **Partial set-off.** Losses from non-speculative business can, on the s.108(1)/s.113(1) wording, be set off against speculation profit, but not the reverse. This is an inference from the one-way restriction in s.113(1).

### Gaps
- **ITR form.** The ITR forms for tax year 2026-27 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026 were not located. Many secondary sources say business income, including intraday, goes on ITR-3 (not ITR-1 or ITR-2). The Memorandum's reference to "ITR-1 & ITR-2" filers keeping 31 July is consistent with this, but I did not verify the form number for tax year 2026-27.
- **Turnover measure.** How "turnover" is computed for speculative trades (commonly said to be the sum of absolute profits and losses, per the ICAI Guidance Note on Tax Audit) was not verified.
- **Presumptive tax and books.** Whether s.58 presumptive taxation can lawfully be used for intraday income is unsettled in commentary, and I found no primary ruling. The s.62 books-of-account thresholds were not checked.
- **Business vs capital gains for CNC trades.** Whether frequent CNC (delivery) trading could be recharacterised as business income was not researched. I did not verify whether the old CBDT Circular 6/2016, which let taxpayers choose capital-gains treatment for listed shares, continues under the 2025 Act.

## Dividends and buyback proceeds: tax rate, TDS, the no-TDS declaration (Form 15G, now Form 121), refunds

### Takeaway
Dividends are slab-rate income for a resident individual: they use the basic exemption and qualify for the rebate.
- **TDS:** 10%, but none if the company pays an individual other than in cash and that company's dividends for the year are Rs 10,000 or less.
- **No-TDS declaration:** a resident individual with nil tax can file a declaration under s.393(6) (reportedly Form 121, which replaced 15G/15H from 1 Apr 2026). Under-60s lose it once the covered income exceeds the basic exemption.
- **Refunds:** any TDS deducted comes back by filing a return.

Buybacks after 1 Apr 2026 are capital gains (cost is deducted from consideration), not dividends. Between the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 change and 31 Mar 2026 they were deemed dividends.

### Cited Findings
- **Buyback was a dividend until 31 Mar 2026.** "Under the existing provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025, consideration received by a shareholder on buy-back of shares by a company is treated as dividend income under section 2(40)(f) ... while the cost of acquisition of the shares extinguished on buy-back is recognised separately as a capital loss under section 69." — [Memorandum, Finance Bill 2026](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf) (PRIMARY, 1 Feb 2026)
- **Buyback is capital gains from 1 Apr 2026.** The 2(40)(f) limb is omitted, and s.69(1) treats "the difference between the cost of acquisition and the value of consideration" as capital gains. The promoter surtax applies only to promoters and only to buybacks under s.68 of the Companies Act. — [Finance Act 2026 ss.35, 42 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf); [Income-tax Act 2025 s.69](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Dividend TDS row, s.393(1) Table Sl. No. 7.** "Any dividend (including dividend on preference shares) declared", payer "Any domestic company", "Rate: 10%", "Threshold limit: Nil". TDS is deducted "before making any distribution or payment of dividend". — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **No TDS on small dividends, s.393(4) Table.** No deduction for "a shareholder, being an individual, if-- (I) the dividend is paid by the company by any mode other than cash; and (II) amount or aggregate of amounts of such dividend distributed or paid or likely to be distributed or paid during the tax year does not exceed 10,000". — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Earlier threshold change.** Under the 1961 Act, Finance Act 2025 s.57 changed "five thousand rupees" to "ten thousand rupees" in the s.194 proviso. — [Finance Act 2025](https://thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Acts/Finance%20Act,%202025.pdf) (PRIMARY). That it took effect on 1 Apr 2025 comes from the Act's general commencement; I did not check it clause by clause.
- **No-TDS declaration, s.393(6).** No TDS where the person furnishes "a written declaration in duplicate in such form and manner as may be prescribed that the tax on such person's estimated total income of the tax year ... shall be nil".
  - The Table covers "An individual being a resident" for items including interest, units, and "(g) dividend referred to in section 393(1) (Table: Sl. No. 7)".
  - The Note bars the declaration (except for residents aged 60+) "if the aggregate of amounts of any income or sum of the nature referred to in provision mentioned in column C ... exceeds the maximum amount not chargeable to tax".
  - Source: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Form 121.** Reportedly a single form for all ages, replacing Forms 15G and 15H under Rule 211 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, from 1 Apr 2026. — [Business Today, 1 Apr 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/income-tax-rules-2026-form-121-simplifies-tds-process-for-senior-citizens-523555-2026-04-01) UNVERIFIED (secondary). The official FAQ at https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/form-121-faqs returned HTTP 403 to me.
- **Surcharge on dividends.** The surcharge on dividend income is capped at 15%, which matters only above Rs 50 lakh of income. — [Memorandum](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf) (PRIMARY)

### Inferences
- **Dividends are taxed at slab rates.** They are not listed in s.196/197/198 (STCG/LTCG), so they are taxed at slab rates, use the basic exemption and qualify for the s.156 rebate. A zero-income individual in the new regime pays nil tax on dividends up to Rs 12 lakh of total income. I did not exhaustively confirm that no special dividend-rate section exists for resident individuals.
- **Small accounts.** A Rs 20,000 portfolio will almost never receive more than Rs 10,000 of dividends from one company, so no TDS arises (if paid electronically) and no Form 121 is needed. If TDS is deducted, filing a return recovers it.
- **Buybacks for a small non-promoter shareholder.** From 1 Apr 2026, gain = consideration − cost. On the text this falls under s.196 or s.198 if the transfer is STT-chargeable, or s.197 or slab rates if not. The shortfall rule and the rebate interplay described above apply. There is no longer a dividend TDS on buyback proceeds.

### Gaps
- Whether STT is charged on buyback tender offers made through the stock-exchange mechanism, which decides s.196/198 vs s.197/slab treatment for a listed buyback, was not verified. Secondary sources state 20% STCG and 12.5% LTCG for listed buybacks: [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/share-buyback-tax-calculation-how-the-finance-bill-2026-proposal-will-boost-shareholder-savings/article-188868) UNVERIFIED (secondary).
- The exact start date of the deemed-dividend regime under the 1961 Act (reportedly 1 Oct 2024, Finance (No. 2) Act 2024) was not opened.
- I did not check whether interest expense is still deductible against dividend income (the old s.57 20% limit) under the 2025 Act.

## If the individual is a minor: clubbing with the parent, the Rs 1,500 exclusion, and account and trading restrictions

### Takeaway
This condition changes everything. If the account holder is under 18, the income (including STCG and intraday) is generally added to the income of the parent with the higher income under s.99(1)(c) and s.99(2)(b). It then uses that parent's basic exemption and shortfall, which an earning parent will already have used up. The Rs 1,500-per-child exclusion (Schedule III Sl. No. 17) is not available under the new regime. Separately, brokers say minor accounts cannot trade intraday or F&O at all (secondary).

### Cited Findings
- **Clubbing, s.99(1)(c).** An individual's total income includes income arising "to the minor child of the such individual". The exceptions are income "(i) on account of work done by such child; or (ii) from activities where his skill, talent, specialised knowledge or experience is applied; or (iii) where such minor child is suffering from disability of the nature specified in section 154". — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY, effective 1 Apr 2026)
- **Which parent, s.99(2)(b).**
  - If the parents' marriage subsists, the income goes to "that parent whose total income before such inclusion is greater".
  - Otherwise, it goes to the parent "who maintains such child during the tax year".
  - Once included, it stays with that parent in later years unless the Assessing Officer is satisfied otherwise.
  - Source: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Rs 1,500 exclusion.** Schedule III Table Sl. No. 17 excludes income includible under s.99(1)(c) "to the extent such income does not exceed 1,500 in respect of each minor child". Section 202(2)(a)(i) removes Sl. No. 17 when computing income under the new regime. — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Account restrictions.** Broker FAQs say a minor's demat/trading account is operated by the guardian, cannot do equity intraday or derivatives, and converts to a regular account at 18 after fresh KYC. Some brokers also restrict buying in minor accounts. — [Zerodha minor account FAQs](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/online-account-opening/minor-account/articles/minor-account-faqs); [FYERS](https://support.fyers.in/portal/en/kb/articles/can-minors-trade-in-futures-and-options-with-fyers); [ShareIndia](https://www.shareindia.com/faqs/what-are-the-trading-restrictions-in-a-minor-account) UNVERIFIED (secondary; none of these links to the SEBI or depository circular).

### Inferences
- **Effect of clubbing.** With an earning parent (income above the basic exemption), the minor's STCG is taxed at 20% plus cess from the first rupee in the parent's hands. LTCG gets only the Rs 1.25 lakh exemption, and intraday profit is taxed at the parent's marginal slab rate. The near-zero tax drag in Scenario A below assumes the account holder is an adult with no or low other income.
- **Character of the income.** The clubbed STCG keeps its character in the parent's hands (s.196 still applies). This is a reading of the text, not confirmed by a ruling I read.
- **Trading as "skill".** Whether a minor's trading profits could fall under the "skill, talent, specialised knowledge" exception is a factual, contested question. I would not assume it.

### Gaps
- The primary SEBI, NSDL or CDSL text on minor-account trading restrictions was not located.
- How clubbing applies in the tax year the child turns 18 (income before vs after the birthday) was not verified.

## Losses, ITR filing, due dates, late fees and advance tax

### Takeaway
- **Filing duty.** A return is compulsory if total income (before Chapter VIII deductions and some exemptions) exceeds the basic exemption limit, even if the tax is nil after the rebate.
- **Losses.** A return is also needed, and must be filed by the due date, to carry forward capital or speculation losses.
- **Exemption.** The shortfall rule is part of the tax computation itself; no separate claim is needed.
- **Due dates for tax year 2026-27:** 31 Jul 2027 if there is no business income; 31 Aug 2027 for intraday or other business income without audit. Belated returns are allowed until 31 Dec 2027, and revised returns until 31 Mar 2028 (fee if filed more than 9 months after the year).
- **Advance tax** is due only if tax for the year is Rs 10,000 or more, so none is due when tax is nil.

### Cited Findings
- **Who must file, s.263(1)(a).**
  - (iii) a person other than a company or firm must file "if his total income ... without giving effect to the provisions of Chapter XVII-B or provisions of Schedule VIII (Table: Sl. No. 1) or deductions allowable under section 82 ... 88 ... or Chapter VIII ... exceeded the maximum amount which is not chargeable to income-tax";
  - (viii) a person "who has sustained a loss in the tax year under the head 'Profits and gains of business or profession' or under the head 'Capital gains' and who intends to claim that such loss ... is to be carried forward";
  - (ix) residents with foreign assets;
  - (x) persons meeting "such conditions as may be prescribed".
  - Source: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Loss rules, ss.108-111 and s.121.**
  - s.108(2)(a): a short-term capital loss is set off against "the income, computed in respect of any other capital asset" (STCG or LTCG).
  - s.108(2)(b): a long-term capital loss is set off only against LTCG.
  - s.111(1)-(2): capital losses are carried forward under the same rules for up to "eight tax years".
  - s.113: speculation losses, 4 years (see the intraday section).
  - s.121: a loss can be carried forward only if it was determined in a return "filed under section 263(1)", that is, the on-time return.
  - Source: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Due dates (s.263(1)(c) as substituted by Finance Act 2026 s.66).**
  - Sl. 1: 30 November (transfer-pricing report cases).
  - Sl. 2: 31 October (companies, audited cases).
  - Sl. 3: 31 August (business or profession income where accounts are not required to be audited).
  - Sl. 4: 31 July ("Any other assessee").
  - Source: [Finance Act 2026 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf) (PRIMARY, effective tax year 2026-27)
- **Belated return, s.263(4).** Allowed "within nine months from the end of the relevant tax year, or before the completion of the assessment, whichever is earlier". — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Revised return, s.263(5) as substituted.** Allowed "subject to the provisions of section 428(b) ... at any time within twelve months from the end of the relevant tax year". — [Finance Act 2026 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Fees, s.428 as substituted.**
  - (a) A person "required to furnish a return ... fails to do so within the due date" pays Rs 1,000 if total income is 5,00,000 or less, and Rs 5,000 otherwise.
  - (b) The same fees apply to a revised return filed "beyond nine months from the end of relevant tax year".
  - (c) Audit failures attract 75,000 / 1,50,000.
  - Source: [Finance Act 2026 s.96 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Advance tax, s.404.** "Advance tax shall be payable by the assessee during a Financial year, where the amount of such tax payable during that year ... is 10000 or more." — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Capital gains arise only on transfer.** Section 67(1) charges "any profits or gains arising from the transfer of a capital asset". — [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)

### Inferences
- **Zero other income, gains only.** Up to Rs 4 lakh of total income, filing is not compulsory under s.263(1)(a)(iii), unless (viii) losses to carry forward, (ix) foreign assets or (x) prescribed conditions apply.
- **Nil tax can still mean compulsory filing.** Intraday profit of Rs 10 lakh with no other income gives nil tax because of the rebate. Filing is still compulsory, because total income exceeds Rs 4 lakh, and the due date is 31 Aug 2027.
- **Losses need an on-time return.** A loss-making first year should be filed by the due date (31 Jul, or 31 Aug with intraday). Otherwise the loss cannot be carried forward (s.121). Filing late only to claim a TDS refund is still possible as a belated return.
- **Advance tax in the tables.** Any table row with tax of Rs 10,000 or more triggers advance tax. Examples: Rs 5 lakh STCG with no other income gives Rs 20,800; Rs 2 lakh STCG with Rs 3 lakh other income gives Rs 20,800. In the Rs 20,000-capital scenarios, tax is nil, so there is no advance tax.
- **Unsold holdings are not taxed.** No tax arises on unrealised gains for an individual investor. Buy-and-hold gains are taxed only when sold: as STCG if held 12 months or less, as LTCG if held more than 12 months.

### Gaps
- The instalment dates and the interest provisions for short or late advance tax under the 2025 Act (old 234B/234C, and the proviso about capital gains arising after an instalment date) were not read.
- The "prescribed conditions" under s.263(1)(a)(x) in the Income-tax Rules, 2026 were not read. Under the 1961 Act they included, for example, TDS above a threshold and high deposits.
- The ITR form numbers for tax year 2026-27 were not verified.
- The status of FY 2025-26 returns under the 1961 Act was not researched. Their due dates (31 Jul / 31 Aug 2026) have passed; the 1961-Act belated deadline is 31 Dec 2026.

## Worked tax tables for small accounts: monthly STCG vs buy-and-hold LTCG vs intraday, and the tax drag

### Takeaway
New regime, resident adult under 60:
- **Scenario A (Rs 20,000 capital, gains Rs 2,000-50,000):** nil tax in every case, whether there is zero other income or Rs 3 lakh. Tax drag is 0% of gains and 0% of capital.
- **Where tax starts:**
  - STCG: above Rs 4 lakh of STCG with zero other income, or above Rs 1 lakh with Rs 3 lakh other income.
  - Equity LTCG: above Rs 5.25 lakh, or Rs 2.25 lakh.
  - Intraday: above Rs 12 lakh, or Rs 9 lakh, because of the rebate.
- **Larger gains:** at Rs 10 lakh of gains, the effective rate is 12.5-18.7% for STCG, 6.2-10.1% for LTCG and 0-7.8% for intraday.
- **Frequency of selling:** realising monthly rather than yearly does not change the tax by itself. What matters is the total for the year, the over-12-month holding test, and timing (unsold gains are deferred).

### Cited Findings
- **Provisions used:**
  - s.202(1) new-regime slabs;
  - s.196(1)-(2) STCG at 20% with the shortfall;
  - s.198(2)-(3), (7) LTCG at 12.5% above Rs 1,25,000, with the shortfall and no rebate;
  - s.156(1)-(3) rebate, capped at slab-rate tax in the new regime;
  - s.2(101)(b) 12-month holding period for listed equity.
  - Source: [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Cess:** 4% under Finance Act 2026 s.3(15). — [Finance Act 2026 (gazette copy)](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/THE-FINANCE-ACT-2026.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Old-regime slabs and rebate:** First Schedule Part I-B and s.156(1). — [Memorandum](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf); [Income-tax Act 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/265620.pdf) (PRIMARY)
- **Calculation:** the figures were produced by the script `r7_scripts/tax_tables.py` in the session scratchpad, run with `python -I`. They are my arithmetic on the provisions above, not an official calculator.

**Formulas (new regime).** O = other slab-rate income (taken as already net taxable, for example interest; any salary standard deduction is ignored). G = gross gain, taken as net of brokerage; STT is not deductible for capital gains, but is deductible for intraday.
- **(i) Monthly-realised STCG (s.196):** Tax = 1.04 × [20% × max(0, G − max(0, 4,00,000 − O)) + slab(O) − rebate]. The rebate is limited to slab(O) by s.156(3).
- **(ii) Buy-and-hold, sold after more than 12 months, LTCG (s.198):** Tax = 1.04 × [12.5% × max(0, G − 1,25,000 − max(0, 4,00,000 − O)) + slab(O) − rebate on slab(O) only].
- **(iii) Intraday speculative income:** Tax = 1.04 × [slab(O + G) − rebate].
  - Rebate = min(slab, 60,000) if O + G ≤ 12,00,000.
  - Above 12 lakh, the rebate is the excess of tax over (O + G − 12,00,000), if positive.

### Inferences
- **Small accounts.** For a Rs 20,000 account run by an adult resident with no or modest other income, income tax adds no drag at any plausible return. The binding costs are STT, brokerage, slippage and other charges, not income tax.
- **Where the treatment starts to matter.**
  - Monthly-realised STCG becomes taxable once annual STCG plus slab income exceeds Rs 4 lakh.
  - Held-over-12-months LTCG gets an extra Rs 1.25 lakh band and a lower 12.5% rate.
  - Intraday income keeps the 12 lakh rebate but is taxed at slab rates above it. It also brings business-return compliance: a 31 Aug due date, likely ITR-3, and losses ring-fenced for 4 years.
- **Losses differ by type.** A short-term capital loss offsets both STCG and LTCG and carries 8 years. A speculation loss offsets only speculation profit and carries 4 years.
- **"Buy and hold" has two meanings.** (a) Selling after more than 12 months gives LTCG, as in column (ii). (b) Not selling gives no tax in the year, a pure deferral. Holding exactly 12 months or less still gives STCG.
- **Mixing types.** Combining intraday and CNC in one account changes the order: intraday (slab) income uses up the Rs 4 lakh exemption before STCG does.
- **Minors.** If the account holder is a minor, the parent's rates apply (see the minors section). The tables above would then generally overstate how much is tax-free.

### Gaps
- The tables ignore surcharge (none below Rs 50 lakh) and any salary standard deduction. They also assume STT-paid transactions, a resident under 60, no Chapter VIII deductions and no carried-forward losses.
- The STCG-before-LTCG ordering of the shortfall is an assumption (see the shortfall section).
- I did not cross-check against the official incometaxindia.gov.in tax calculator for tax year 2026-27.
